If you walk into my room, the first thing you'll notice isn't me. It's the walls. Almost every inch is covered in posters: films, series, characters and music, from the floor right up to the ceiling. In the evening, with the lamp on, the whole room glows orange and red, like the inside of a cinema just before the lights go down.

It didn't start as a plan. It started with one poster.

## One story at a time

The first one went up because I loved that story and wanted to see it every day. Then another film stayed with me, so it went up too. Then another. A wall like this doesn't fill up in a weekend. It fills up the way a life does, one thing you love at a time.

At some point I stepped back and realised the wall had turned into a kind of diary. I can point at almost any poster and tell you where I was in life when that story found me.

![The full wall of posters from floor to ceiling](/assets/blog/my-wall-of-stories/whole-wall.jpg "Some mornings the room is also my gym. The posters don't count my reps, which I appreciate.")

## Why stories matter to me

Long before I made anything of my own, stories were how I made sense of the world. A good film can make you brave for a week. A good series can make strangers feel like old friends. A good album can carry you through a hard month.

Looking at the wall every day reminds me that people made all of this. Writers who rewrote a scene one more time at 3 a.m. Composers who threw away a hundred melodies to keep one. Designers who spent days on a single poster so a stranger would stop and look. I wanted to be part of that, in my own small way.

> Every poster on that wall is a thank-you note to someone who made something I loved.

## My swipe file, on a wall

In [What a Small Book Taught Me](/blog/small-book), I wrote about collecting the things that move you. This wall is the biggest version of that. It's my swipe file, printed and pinned up where I can't ignore it.

When I'm planning a shot, I'll look up and borrow a colour from one poster, a mood from another, the way a character stands from a third. None of it gets copied. It gets mixed, the way a song you love quietly shapes the next beat you make. The wall doesn't tell me what to make. It reminds me what I love, and that's usually enough to get started.

## The room where things get made

There's a microphone in one corner with a pop filter in front of it, a lamp in another, and a row of shoeboxes along the floor. Someone even mounted a couple of toy blasters above the posters. (It was me. I'm not sorry.)

This is where I write beats, plan shots for ad films, sketch designs and edit videos. Some mornings it's where I train, too. It's a lot for one room. But surrounded by all those stories, it rarely feels like work. It feels like joining a long conversation that started well before me.

## A wall is never finished

A wall like this is never really done. There's always one more story that deserves a spot, even if it means overlapping a corner or two. Maybe a film that comes out next year. Maybe something I make myself.

That's my favourite thought of all: that one day, someone might put up a poster of something I made. One small story, on somebody else's wall of stories.

### Little things I noticed

- At night, the lamp makes the red posters look like they're glowing from the inside.
- My best ideas show up when I'm staring at the wall, not at a screen.
- Red light makes everything in the room look like a film still.
- Every poster is a story, and together they've started telling mine.
