There's a kind of light you only get for a few minutes a day. The sun drops low, slips between the trees and lands on the road ahead in long, warm streaks. Everything it touches turns gold for a little while: the tarmac, the leaves, the edge of a matte black tank.

The photo at the top is dressed up a little. The real light was softer, more like the pictures further down. But it's exactly how that moment felt, and that's sort of the point of this post. Sometimes you have to picture the light before it's all the way there, and then ride toward it.

Riding has a few simple rules. The longer I think about them, the more they sound like rules for everything else.

## You go where you look

Ask anyone who rides and they'll tell you: the bike goes where your eyes go. Stare at the pothole and you'll drift straight into it. Look at the gap in the traffic, the curve, the open road beyond it, and your hands quietly steer you there.

I catch myself staring at potholes all the time, and almost never on a bike. A beat with one wrong note. A shoot with one bad take. A rep that didn't go to plan. I give the mistake all my attention, then wonder why I keep ending up there. The fix is simple, even if it isn't easy: look up. Keep your eyes on the thing you want to make, and let your hands catch up.

## Balance comes from moving

Two wheels are at their wobbliest when you're barely moving. At walking pace, every tiny correction feels huge. Give the bike a little speed and it steadies itself, as if the road is doing half the balancing for you.

Starting anything feels like those first slow metres. The first week of training, when every movement feels clumsy. The first video, when you hear your own voice and want to delete it. The first song, which sounds nothing like the one in your head. The wobble isn't a sign to stop. It only means you haven't built up any speed yet. Keep going, and one day you'll notice it's gone. You won't even remember when it stopped.

![Ignatius sitting on the black Jawa by the roadside, smiling at the camera](/assets/blog/ride/parked.jpg "The tank is matte black, so it never glares. It just drinks the light in.")

> The wobble isn't a sign to stop. It only means you haven't built up any speed yet.

## The road is never empty

If you wait for a perfectly clear road, you'll never leave. There's always a car pulling out, a cart rolling past, someone crossing without looking. Riding teaches you to go anyway: carefully, eyes open, but go.

Plans work the same way. There will always be a reason to wait: a better camera, a quieter month, more confidence, more time. The novelist E. L. Doctorow once compared writing to driving at night. You can only see as far as your headlights, he said, but you can make the whole trip that way. You don't need to see the whole road. You only need to see the next bit, and move.

![Ignatius on the Jawa, looking back at a stone wall painted with bright flowers](/assets/blog/ride/look-back.jpg "Someone painted flowers on this old stone wall. I couldn't stop looking back at them.")

## Golden hour doesn't wait

Golden hour lasts minutes, not hours. You can't book it, and you can't save it for later. It shows up for whoever is already out on the road.

So here's my wish for you this week. Pick the thing you've been circling: the song, the shoot, the first workout, the message you keep drafting and deleting. Start the engine. Look where you want to go, not at the potholes. Expect the first few metres to wobble. And ride toward the light, even if you can only see a little of it from here.
